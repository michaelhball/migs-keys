**Attentive slow practice.** Pick a short passage and one question, such as “Can I move from E to G without interrupting the beat?” Before playing, identify the notes, rhythm, and fingering. Imagine or sing the sound if you can. Play slowly enough to prepare each movement, and listen to the result.

After an attempt, name what happened: a wrong note, a late note, an uneven sound, or uncomfortable movement. Adjust one thing and try again. Once the passage feels repeatable, practise something else and return to it. At your next session, try it before reviewing.

Slow practice should preserve the rhythm and musical shape. As a passage becomes secure, we will try modestly faster versions and, when useful, tiny groups at a more natural speed. [Graham Fitch explains slow practice and the transition to faster playing here.](https://practisingthepiano.com/the-three-ss-part-two/)

**Overlapping sections.** A measure, also called a bar, is a group of beats. Imagine a piece with four measures, A, B, C, and D. Work on A plus the beginning of B; then B plus the beginning of C; then C plus the beginning of D. Finally join longer spans. Including the next beginning gives you practice crossing each join.

For a very short melody we can overlap by notes instead. In Lesson 1, notes 1 through 5 overlap notes 4 through 8. Preserve the original beat count when starting in the middle. Once each group is comfortable, join the full phrase.

If a join still catches, practise just the last two notes before it and the first two after it. This exercise applies Fitch's overlapping-section method described in the linked article.
