## What this version contains

This is a complete beginner arrangement of **Simple Gifts**, the traditional tune used for **Lord of the Dance**. It contains both eight-bar sections. Familiar sung versions can differ in repeated notes and rhythm; this uses the traditional tune with its original Simple Gifts words. The lyric highlight follows the current phrase as you play or step through the music. Sydney Carter’s modern lyrics and adaptation are separate: [read the authorised Lord of the Dance lyrics at the publisher](https://stainer.co.uk/lord-of-the-dance/).

Choose **Organ** beside playback, then select **Right hand** and **Opening**. Start around **45–60 quarter notes per minute**. Amazing Grace is the easier of these two arrangements if the eighth notes feel too quick.

## Find your starting notes

The right thumb plays the two pickup **G3s**, below middle C. Then move the thumb to **C4 (middle C)** for the first full bar. From C4, fingers 1–5 cover C D E F G. The melody sometimes reaches below this position: follow the displayed fingers and move the hand instead of stretching to keep the thumb on C.

The left little finger (5) starts on **C3**, one octave below middle C, after the pickup. It moves to **G2** for the other bass note. G2 is the white key immediately right of F2, below C3. Release the previous note before moving. The left hand is silent during the pickup.

## Rhythm

Count **1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &** in each full bar. A quarter note lasts one numbered beat; each eighth note lasts half a beat. The two pickup Gs enter on **4 &** before bar 1.

In bar 9, a half note uses beats 1–2. The dotted quarter uses beat 3 through the first half of beat 4; the final eighth enters on **4 &**. A dot adds half the original note’s duration. The final bar lasts three beats, complementing the one-beat pickup.

## Practice

1. Learn the pickup and bar 1 with the right hand. Count aloud; keep the four eighth notes at the end even.
2. Add bar 2, then practise the join from bar 2 into bar 3. Learn the rest of the first eight bars in two-bar groups.
3. Work on bars 9–12 separately. Clap the dotted rhythm before playing it. Use Previous/Next to inspect a change without waiting for playback.
4. Learn bars 13–16. Join bars 12–13 before playing the second section through.
5. Practise the bass alone. Each whole note lasts four beats; the final dotted half lasts three. In bass clef, C3 is the second space and G2 is the bottom line.
6. Combine one bar at a time. Keep the bass held while the right hand moves. On organ, release at the written end of each note; avoid using the sustain pedal for this study.

## Sources

Joseph Brackett’s **Simple Gifts** dates from 1848. [American Music Preservation](https://www.americanmusicpreservation.com/JosephBrackettSimpleGifts.htm) documents the tune and its later use by Sydney Carter. Melody reference: [Simple Gifts, John Chambers ABC collection](https://abcnotation.com/tunePage?a=trillian.mit.edu%2F~jc%2Fmusic%2Fabc%2Fmirror%2Ffolkinfo.org%2FSimple_Gifts%2F0000). The melody is transposed to C; accompaniment and beginner fingerings were prepared for this course.

For the rhythms, see [Musictheory.net: note duration](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/11) and [dots and ties](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/14).
