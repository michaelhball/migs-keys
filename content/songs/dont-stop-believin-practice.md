## What is included

This is a **116-bar piano accompaniment** based on the [Pianote tutorial you requested](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=amRtNe2uVwE): intro, verses, instrumental sections, the “Strangers” section, chorus and ending loop. Both hands are included. It is an accompaniment, so the sung melody will not play above it.

Repeated riffs use consistent voicings and bass approaches for practice. The ending loop plays once; choose **Ending loop** and **Repeat passage** to keep it going. This is a practice arrangement, not an exact transcription of every variation in the video.

## Start with the intro

Select **Intro · bars 1–8**, then **Right hand**. Start at 40–60 quarter notes per minute. Use **Piano view** to step through without audio, or **Sheet music** to follow the moving dots.

1. Find B3 just below middle C, then E4 and B4 above it. Alternate E4 + B4 together with the lower B3. Suggested fingers are 2 + 5, then 1. Allow the hand to move; do not keep it stretched.
2. Count **1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**. The pair falls on each number; the lower note falls on each “&”. Practise one bar, then the change into the next chord.
3. Choose **Left hand**. The bass begins on E2. Hold the first note through “1 & 2 & 3”, then play F♯2 on “&”, G♯2 on “4”, and B2 on the last “&”. That B continues into the next bar.
4. A curved **tie** joins the same pitch across the bar line: keep holding it. The bass does not restart simply because a new bar begins.
5. Combine only the first bar at first. Then practise the last two beats of bar 1 and first two beats of bar 2. Extend the section once that join is clear.

The key is E major: F, C, G and D are sharp unless the score says otherwise. A sharp raises a note by one semitone, to the next key on the piano, including black keys.

## The chord section

Select **Strangers · bars 41–56**. The right hand initially plays short chords on **1** and the **& after 2**, followed by silence. The left hand plays steady eighth notes. Practise the right-hand rhythm on one chord before combining the hands.

The left-hand part uses octaves. If you cannot reach them comfortably, play just the lower note of each pair on your keyboard. The app plays the written octave. Release between repeated notes and avoid holding the hand rigidly open.

## Working through the arrangement

Use the named sections to navigate the song. The shorter **Practice bars** selections overlap by one bar, so you can rehearse joins. You can also choose a bar in Piano view, move one position at a time, and start Listen from that position.

This song is a longer-term project alongside the beginner lessons. It introduces black keys, hand movement and independent rhythms before those have all been covered in the course. The first right-hand bar is a reasonable starting point; you do not need to learn the whole arrangement now.
