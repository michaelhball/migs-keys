# Piano course plan

The course starts with keyboard notes and reading music, then develops technique, rhythm, chords, accompaniment, and improvisation. Jazz, blues, classical, pop, and synthesizer exercises are introduced as the required skills develop.

Work on one lesson at a time. A lesson can take several practice sessions. Use the checkpoints and practice notes to decide whether to repeat it, simplify an exercise, or move on. The order can change when a different style or piece is more useful.

## Lesson structure

Each lesson contains:

- A specific skill to learn.
- An explanation and playing example.
- Instructions for practising difficult sections.
- A reading, listening, or improvisation exercise.
- Checkpoints to try again in a later session.

## Course sequence

| Lessons | Topics |
| --- | --- |
| 1–6 | Keyboard notes, rhythm, treble and bass clefs, hand coordination, and an eight-measure piece |
| 7–12 | Scales, intervals, chords, inversions, phrasing, playing by ear, and transposing |
| 13–19 | Seventh chords, 12-bar blues, swing, improvisation, jazz progressions, and lead sheets |
| 20–24 | Classical reading, pop accompaniment, backing tracks, synthesizers, and repertoire review |

The complete lesson list is in `content/course.json`. Lesson 1 is written; the remaining lessons are planned. New lessons will use the learner's practice notes and check-ins to set the difficulty.

## Practice schedule

| Minutes | Task |
| --- | --- |
| 3 | Recall a previous exercise before checking the notes |
| 5 | Work on the current reading, rhythm, or chord exercise |
| 10 | Practise the assigned piece in short, overlapping sections |
| 5 | Play by ear, improvise, or apply the exercise to a piece |
| 2 | Record difficulties and the next practice target |

For a ten-minute session, use two minutes for recall, five for one difficult section, and three for playing or improvising. Video demonstrations count as part of the lesson time; pause and try each demonstrated movement.

## Practice and review

Choose a tempo that lets you prepare each movement and hear errors. Identify the specific problem after an attempt, adjust one thing, and try again. Practise the transition into the next section so separate groups can be joined without pausing. See `content/practice-method.md` for examples.

At a check-in, report what you practised, which parts were reliable, where you hesitated, and what you want to work on next. Use this information to revise the assignment. Current progress is stored in `data/progress.json`.

External videos and books are used for specific demonstrations or pieces. Source links are listed in the app's Resources view. The exercises provided locally are enough to begin; books and paid courses are optional.
