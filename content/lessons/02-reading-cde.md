## Your goal

Read C, D, and E on the treble staff and connect each written note to its key. You can start this lesson today. The suggested practice times are guides, not a daily limit.

## Three positions to recognise

A staff has five horizontal lines and four spaces between them. Count both from the bottom. The treble clef is also called the G clef: its curl marks G4 on the second line from the bottom. We will use that G later. Read a note’s pitch from the oval notehead, not the tip of its stem.

- **Middle C (C4)** sits on a short extra line below the staff. That short line is called a ledger line.
- **D4** sits in the space just below the bottom staff line, between middle C and E.
- **E4** sits on the bottom staff line.

In the player above, choose **Sheet music**. Find the bottom line first. Then identify notes on that line, just below it, or on the extra line. Higher positions here mean higher-sounding keys, to the right on the keyboard. The small numbers are finger suggestions, not pitch names.

## Practise · about 15–20 minutes

1. **Locate the keys.** Put right-hand fingers 1, 2, and 3 on middle C, D, and E. On keyboards without octave labels, use the C nearest the physical centre as a starting guide; ask me if its octave is unclear.
2. **Read without playing.** Say the first four note names from the staff. Use Piano view and its Next button to check the highlighted keys, then return to the staff.
3. **Play four notes slowly.** Begin with E, using finger 3. Look at the next written note before playing it. If you need to stop, stop; choose a slower pulse on the next attempt.
4. **Read the second bar.** Identify each note by its position. Play the four notes, then try the two bars together.
5. **Practise the join.** Work on notes 3–6: C, D, C, C. Count “3, 4, 1, 2”. Then start one note earlier and finish one note later before returning to the whole phrase.
6. **Check reading rather than memory.** Point to a note in the middle of the staff and play just that note. Try several positions in a different order.

All notes in this exercise last one beat. The 4/4 marking means four quarter-note beats in each bar. A filled oval with a stem is a quarter note. The vertical line divides the bars.

## Pulse and listening

Use a metronome if it helps. You do not need to reproduce an exact BPM from memory. The task is to keep the notes evenly spaced at a manageable speed. Listen once, then try playing with the metronome alone.

## Make a variation

Change the first note from E to C. Play both versions and listen to the difference. Point to where that new C would sit on the staff.

## Check and continue

Try identifying these three staff positions again after a short break. If reading is slower than playing from memory, that is the part to practise. Record any recurring mix-up in your journal.
