## Your goal

Play an eight-bar accompaniment using **C–Am–F–G**, repeated twice. Each chord lasts one bar. This is an accompaniment pattern you can later use under a melody or singing.

## Add F and G

All four right-hand shapes are in root position, with fingers **1–3–5**:

| Chord | Right-hand keys | Left-hand bass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C | C4 E4 G4 | C3 |
| Am | A3 C4 E4 | A2 |
| F | F4 A4 C5 | F2 |
| G | G4 B4 D5 | G2 |

Move the whole right hand between shapes. The new high note **D5** is on the **fourth treble line**. G major here means the chord G–B–D; we are not learning its full scale yet.

## Place the left hand

Start with little finger **5 on C3**, as in Lesson 4. For this exercise, move the whole left hand and use 5 for each bass note. Do not stretch to leave it on C3.

**A2** is two white-key steps left of C3. **F2** is farther left, immediately before a group of three black keys; **G2** is its next white key. In bass clef: **F2 is the space below the staff; G2 is the bottom line; A2 is the bottom space; C3 is the second space**. Find all four before starting.

## Rhythm

Play bass and chord together on **1**, hold through **2**, then release and play the same bass and chord again on **3**. Hold through **4**. Change to the next chord on the following 1. The two half notes fill each 4/4 bar.

## Practise · about 25 minutes

1. Learn the right-hand F and G shapes separately. Play their notes broken, then together. Read each shape before checking the piano view.
2. Play the right-hand sequence C–Am–F–G. Pause between chords while learning the moves; then try two half notes per bar at a slow steady pace.
3. Play the left bass sequence alone. Look toward the destination before moving.
4. Combine C for one bar. Then combine C and Am for two bars. Keep the bass quieter than the right hand on acoustic piano.
5. Practise the harder joins **Am→F** and **G→C** separately. Include beats 3–4 of the old chord and beats 1–2 of the new one. Release before moving; a small gap is acceptable while learning.
6. Join the four-bar loop, then play both repetitions. Do not speed up until you can prepare the largest move in time.

## Try another sound

Select Electric piano or Organ. On organ, the sound holds at a steady level until release, so listen for clean changes. Velocity may not change its loudness like an acoustic piano. The player’s sound choice changes the demonstration, not your keyboard’s internal sound.

[Musictheory.net: chords](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/40) can help you recognise the stacked-note shapes. Lesson 10 will reduce the right-hand movement without changing these four chords.

## Check and continue

Play a complete four-bar loop with no unplanned pauses. If moving both hands at once is still difficult, keep practising two-bar groups before increasing speed.
