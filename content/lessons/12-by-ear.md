## Your goal

Find a short phrase by ear, then play the first four bars of Ode to Joy in two different positions. This combines listening, reading and a practical introduction to **transposition**.

## Listen before looking

Rest your right hand over C4–G4, with thumb on C. Listen to the first phrase from Lesson 11 without watching its keyboard. Find the opening E, then try the next two notes. Ask whether each note repeats, rises or falls. Check the score only after making an attempt; work with two or three notes at a time.

If you already remember all four bars, sing or hum the phrase and then play it. The aim is to connect the sound you intend with the keys, not to test whether you have forgotten the score.

## Move the whole phrase

Put your right thumb on **G4**, with fingers 2–5 over **A4 B4 C5 D5**. The new first note is **B4**, still played with finger 3. Keep the same rhythm and finger pattern as the phrase in C.

The correspondence is **C→G, D→A, E→B, F→C, G→D**. All these notes are white keys. A full G-major scale also contains F♯ (F sharp, the black key immediately right of F), but this five-note phrase never reaches that note; you do not need that scale today.

**Transposing** means moving every pitch by the same interval, preserving the melody’s pitch relationships and rhythm. An **interval** is the distance between pitches. C to G spans five letter names, counting C and G: C D E F G. This move is a perfect fifth upward, or seven semitones. You only need this example now, not a table of every interval.

## Practise · about 20–25 minutes

1. Find the first phrase by ear in C. Use a short section, then compare with Lesson 11.
2. In this player, **bars 1–4** show that phrase in C. Read and play it without the finger numbers.
3. Move the thumb to G4. Play B B C5 D5, the first bar of the new version. Then check the score.
4. **Bars 5–8** show the same phrase in G. Keep its dotted rhythm unchanged. B4 is on the treble middle line, C5 in the third space and D5 on the fourth line.
5. Alternate the two versions, pausing to reposition between them. The pause is for this comparison; the demonstration plays them consecutively at the written tempo.
6. Invent a two-bar phrase using C D E F G, then move it to G A B C D. Keep the rhythm simple enough that you can hear whether the shape stayed the same.

[Musictheory.net: generic intervals](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/30) explains counting letter names. Concentrate on steps, thirds and the C-to-G fifth; interval qualities will receive more practice later.

## Check and review

Can you play both versions starting from the correct E or B, and hear that the second version is higher while retaining the same melody? At your next session, try to recover the G version before checking the score.

This completes the second lesson block. Review one accompaniment from Lessons 9–10 and one melody from Lessons 7 or 11. Amazing Grace and Simple Gifts are optional song projects; you can take just their first phrase while these skills settle.
