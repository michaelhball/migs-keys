## Your goal

Keep counting while a note continues and while there is silence. Use the same right-hand C, D, and E as before.

## What changes on the page

In this 4/4 exercise, a **quarter note** (filled head and stem) lasts one beat. A **half note** (open head and stem) lasts two beats. A **half rest** is a small rectangle sitting on the middle staff line; it means two beats of silence.

The first bar is C for one beat, D for one beat, then E for two beats. The second bar is D for one beat, C for one beat, then two silent beats. The numbered groups in Note names show which counts belong to each event.

## Practise · about 15 minutes

1. **Count without keys.** Count “1, 2, 3, 4” twice. Tap on 1, 2, and 3 in the first bar, then on 1 and 2 in the second. Keep counting on the other beats without another tap.
2. **Play the first bar.** Use fingers 1, 2, and 3 for C, D, and E. Press E on beat 3, keep it down through beat 4, and release it as the next bar starts.
3. **Play the second bar.** Play D on 1 and C on 2. Release C on 3 and keep counting “3, 4” in silence.
4. **Join the bars.** Practise the held E followed by D and C. Count “3, 4, 1, 2”. Listen for the change from one held note to separate notes.
5. **Repeat after the rest.** Keep the metronome running through the silence, then restart on the next 1. If you lose the count, tap the rhythm again before adding pitches.

Choose a slow tempo you can count comfortably. A longer note is held; it is not struck again on every click. Use Listen to check the rhythm, then try with only the metronome.

## Change one duration

Play four Cs, one per beat. Then play two Cs, each lasting two beats. Both fill one bar. Listen to how fewer attacks change the phrase even though the pulse stays the same.

## Check and continue

Play the two-bar exercise while counting aloud, including the final rest. If the silence becomes shorter when you repeat, practise the last C, the two silent beats, and the first C of the next repetition as one group.

Log what needed attention, then continue to Lesson 4 if you want to. The next lesson uses the left hand separately.
