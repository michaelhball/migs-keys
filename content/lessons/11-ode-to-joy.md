## Your goal

Play the eight-bar opening of Beethoven’s **Ode to Joy** theme in C, including its dotted rhythm. This is the melody alone, not the full symphonic work. It uses the five-note position from Lesson 6, leaving your attention free for rhythm and phrasing.

## Set up

Right thumb **1 on C4**, then 2 on D4, 3 on E4, 4 on F4, and 5 on G4. The first note is **E4**, played with finger 3. The left hand rests throughout.

## Two eighth notes share a beat

In 4/4, each quarter note lasts one beat. An **eighth note** lasts half a beat. Count the subdivisions **1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &**. An eighth has a filled head, stem and one flag; neighbouring eighth notes may share one beam instead of separate flags. Beams do not change their duration.

A dot adds half the original note value. A **dotted quarter** lasts **1½ beats**, or three eighth notes. In bar 4, play E on **1**, hold through **1 & 2**, play D on **2 &**, then play D again on **3**, holding through **4**. The D on 2 & and the D on 3 are separate notes, so release and play again. Bar 8 uses the same rhythm on D, C, C.

[Musictheory.net: note duration](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/11) and [dots and ties](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/14) show these shapes. Focus on eighth notes and the single dot today.

## Practise · about 25 minutes

1. Say the notes of bars 1–2, then play them. Every note there is a quarter note.
2. Clap bar 4’s attacks while counting eight subdivisions: clap on **1**, **2 &**, and **3**. Keep counting while holding or remaining silent.
3. Play bar 4 on the piano. Check that the first E is three times as long as the short D which follows it.
4. Learn bars 3–4, then join bars 2–3. Play the first four bars at a steady slow tempo.
5. Learn bars 5–8. Bars 5–7 repeat bars 1–3; the final bar changes the ending. Read that difference rather than relying on muscle memory.
6. Join bars 4–5, then play all eight bars. Keep the half notes long enough and avoid accelerating through repeated notes.

## Shape a phrase

A **phrase** is a connected musical idea, often moving toward an arrival or pause. Treat bars 1–4 as one phrase and bars 5–8 as its answer. Try slightly more weight near the middle of each phrase and a quieter ending, while keeping the rhythm intact. The demonstration uses a steady tempo; it does not model every expressive choice.

A familiar hymn uses this theme too: **Joyful, Joyful, We Adore Thee**. [Hymnary’s HYMN TO JOY page](https://hymnary.org/tune/hymn_to_joy_beethoven) identifies the Beethoven tune; hymn arrangements can differ in rhythm and key.

## Check and continue

Play bar 4 correctly on its own, then start from bar 3 and continue through bar 5. If that join is reliable, play the full melody. Keep finger numbers off unless you need help deciding a movement.
