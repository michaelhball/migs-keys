## Your goal

Play three notes together and move between **C major** and **A minor**. A three-note chord built by stacking two thirds is a **triad**. A third spans three letter names, such as C–D–E; it skips the middle letter when played as C–E.

## Place the right hand

For **C major**, put fingers **1–3–5 on C4–E4–G4**. You met these notes as a broken chord in Lesson 6. Now sound all three together. Rest the unused fingers near D and F.

For **A minor (Am)**, move the entire hand left: **1–3–5 on A3–C4–E4**. A3 is two white-key steps left of middle C: C4, B3, A3. On the treble staff, A3 is on the **second ledger line below** the staff. Do not leave the thumb at C while reaching for A.

The **root** names the chord: C for C major, A for A minor. With that root as the lowest note, these shapes are in **root position**. “Major” and “minor” describe different chord structures; listen to their different sound without assuming every major chord must sound happy or every minor chord sad.

## Read a chord

Noteheads stacked above each other are played together. They share a stem and duration. Each chord here is a **half note**, followed by a **half rest**: play on 1, hold through 2, release on 3, and remain silent through 4. Use that silence to move and prepare.

## Practise · about 20 minutes

1. Play C, E and G separately, then together. Listen for whether one note arrives late. Try again at a comfortable volume with the fingers already touching their keys.
2. Release and move to A–C–E. Play it separately, then together. Keep the wrist comfortable.
3. Read bars 1–4: C, C, Am, Am. Count **play, hold, rest, prepare** over 1–2–3–4. Do not count each note in a chord as another beat.
4. Read bars 5–8: C, Am, Am, C. Rehearse the move from bar 5 to 6 and bar 7 to 8. The chord and the rest must each last two beats.
5. Join bars 4–5, then play all eight bars. The left hand can rest throughout this exercise.

## Listen

Alternate C major and A minor without watching the score. Say which you are about to play, then listen. If you have a way to hide your hands from view, play one and try to recognise its sound; do not judge yourself on perfect recognition yet.

Try playing each chord broken, then together. The pitches stay the same; only the way you present them changes.

[Musictheory.net: introduction to chords](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/40) gives the larger picture. For now, retain the two shapes **C–E–G** and **A–C–E**. Diminished and augmented chords can wait.

## Check and continue

Can you find both shapes without the player, start their notes together, and release for the full rest? If so, Lesson 9 adds two more major chords and a simple bass part.
