## Your goal

Play one octave of C major, then use its notes in an eight-bar melody. This is the first lesson in which the right hand moves out of its starting position. Work without the left hand.

## Notes and starting position

Put right thumb **1 on C4**, middle C. A **scale** is an ordered sequence of pitches. C major uses **C D E F G A B C**, all white keys. C5 is the next C to the right of C4, an octave higher.

The new treble-staff landmarks are **A4: second space**, **B4: middle line**, and **C5: third space**. Count from the bottom. Read the noteheads first; the **Show fingering** button is available if you need a reminder.

## Cross the thumb

Ascending fingering is **1 2 3, 1 2 3 4 5**. Play C D E, then move the thumb to F as your hand travels right. Let the elbow and forearm follow the hand; do not hold the fingers over their old keys or twist the wrist to reach F.

Descending, play **5 4 3 2 1, 3 2 1**. After thumb on F, bring finger 3 to E while moving the hand left. First try just D–E–F–G and G–F–E–D. Stop if the movement feels strained; a small lift and reposition is better than forcing a smooth connection.

## Practise · about 25 minutes

1. **Recall Lesson 6.** Read and play its first two bars once. Check F and G before adding more notes.
2. **Player bars 1–4: the scale.** Bars 1–2 go up; bars 3–4 come down. The top C is played twice, once at each direction's start. Begin without a metronome. Aim for even volume and an unhurried thumb crossing.
3. **Player bars 5–8: melody, first half.** Learn two bars at a time. At the end of bar 8, D is a half note; hold through counts 3–4.
4. **Player bars 9–12: melody, second half.** Bar 9 uses C–E–G with 1–3–5. Before bar 10, move the whole hand so the thumb can play F4; the notes there are F–A–C5 with 1–3–5. This is a position change, not a stretch from C.
5. **Join bars 8–9 and 9–10.** Practise the final note of one bar into the first two notes of the next. Include the full held duration before moving.
6. **Play bars 5–12 as one melody.** Start slowly enough for the position changes. Then try starting directly at bar 9 to check that you know the second half independently.

## Hear and read

Play C4, then C5. They share a note name but differ in height. Now play C D E F G A B C slowly and sing or hum along if comfortable. The scale sounds settled on its final C.

A **semitone** is the distance to the next keyboard key, including black keys. A **whole tone** is two semitones. E–F and B–C are the neighbouring white-key semitones. The major-scale pattern is **whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half**; “half step” means semitone. You do not need to build scales in other keys yet.

[Musictheory.net: major scales](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/21) shows this pattern. Focus on C major and then return to the keyboard.

## Check and continue

Find A4, B4 and C5 from the staff without finger numbers. Play the scale slowly, then the eight-bar melody. Log the specific crossing or position change that needs another session. Recheck it tomorrow, even if you continue to Lesson 8 today.
