## Your goal

Play a right-hand melody while the left hand adds one bass note at the start of each bar. The two keyboard displays show the hands separately.

## Set up

Put right-hand fingers 1, 2, and 3 on C4, D4, and E4. Put the left little finger (5) on C3, the C one octave below middle C.

The top staff is the right hand and the bottom staff is the left hand. Notes lined up vertically start together. A quarter rest on the lower staff means the left hand stays silent for that beat while the right hand continues.

In this exercise the left C lasts **one beat**. Release it on beat 2. We will add longer held bass notes later.

## Practise · about 20 minutes

1. **Right hand alone.** Play the melody from Lesson 1 once: C D E D, E D C C. Choose a pace that leaves time to think about the other hand.
2. **Left hand alone.** Count four beats. Play C3 on 1, release it on 2, then count 2, 3, 4 with no left-hand sound. Repeat.
3. **Both hands, one beat.** Play C3 and C4 together, then release both. Repeat a few times without a metronome. Listen for one combined attack, rather than two separate starts.
4. **First two beats.** Play both Cs on 1. On 2, release the left C while the right hand plays D. Pause and reset. This release is the new coordination task.
5. **First bar.** Add right-hand E and D on 3 and 4. Keep the left hand quiet. Then practise the second bar separately, starting with left C3 and right E4 together.
6. **Join the bars.** Practise right-hand E, D, E, D across counts 3, 4, 1, 2. Add left C3 only on 1. Then try the whole phrase.

If both hands stop when the left hand releases, practise only beats 1–2 again. Say “together, right” while you play. Add the remaining beats once that movement feels clear.

## Change the sound

Try making the left C quieter than the right-hand melody. Listen to whether the melody remains easy to follow. Keep the timing unchanged.

## Check and continue

Play both bars twice at a comfortable tempo. Check that the left hand starts only on beat 1 and releases on beat 2. Record which transition needs more work. Lesson 6 applies this same coordination to a longer piece.
