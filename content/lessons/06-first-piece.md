## Your goal

Learn an original eight-bar study in sections. Add F and G to the right-hand notes you already know, while keeping the left-hand part simple.

## New notes

Keep right-hand thumb on C4. Finger 2 rests on D, 3 on E, 4 on **F**, and 5 on **G**. On the treble staff, F4 sits in the bottom space and G4 is on the second line from the bottom.

Practise C D E F G and back once, without a fixed tempo. Let the fingers rest near the keys. The left hand plays C3 with finger 5 on beat 1 of each bar, then releases on beat 2, as in Lesson 5.

## Read the shape first

Choose Sheet music. Find repeated notes, steps to neighbouring staff positions, and skips. In the first bar, E to G skips over F. In bar 5, C to E to G makes a broken C-major chord: its notes are played separately.

All notes in this study are quarter notes, one per beat. The left hand has three quarter rests after each C. Count four beats in every bar.

## Practise · about 20–25 minutes

1. **Bars 1–2, right hand.** Say the notes, then play. If the move from G at the end of bar 1 to F at the start of bar 2 causes a pause, practise E G F E across that boundary.
2. **Add the left hand to bars 1–2.** Place its C only on each 1. Keep it quieter than the melody. If coordination breaks down, work on one bar before rejoining them.
3. **Bars 3–4.** Learn the right hand first, then add the same left-hand pattern. Compare the ending of bar 4 with the ending of bar 2.
4. **Join bars 2–3.** Practise the last two notes of bar 2 and the first two of bar 3. Then start at the beginning of bar 2 and continue through bar 3.
5. **Bars 5–6, then 7–8.** Repeat the same process. You can leave these for another session if attention is fading.
6. **Play all eight bars.** Use a tempo at which the hardest join is manageable. Notice the first place that becomes uncertain and work on that short section before another full run.

Do not increase speed just because one section is easy. If you want to play faster, change the tempo a little and check whether the joins remain even.

## Make a musical choice

Play bars 1–4 at one comfortable volume, then bars 5–8 a little quieter. Alternatively, keep the melody clear and make every bass C quieter. Choose one change at a time and listen to the result.

## Check and review

Try starting at bar 3 or bar 5, rather than always at the beginning. Then play the whole study and note one specific next target: a reading mix-up, a pause, or a note that is too loud.
