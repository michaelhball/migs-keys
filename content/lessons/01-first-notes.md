## Your goal

Find C, D, and E, keep a steady beat, and practise an eight-note melody. Work through the lesson over two or three sessions.

Choose a plain piano sound. Sit with supported feet, relaxed shoulders, and your forearms approximately level with the keys. Let your fingers have a comfortable natural curve and your wrists move freely. Adjust your seat or keyboard position if reaching feels awkward. If playing hurts, stop and adjust rather than repeating the movement. Comfortable coordinated movement will remain part of our lessons; [Penelope Roskell's foundational technique work](https://www.roskellacademy.com/for-pianists/foundations-in-piano-technique/) is one reference for that part of the course.

## First session · about 20 minutes

1. **Find the notes for three minutes.** Find groups of two black keys and groups of three. The white key immediately to the left of a group of two is C. Find several Cs. To the right of C, the next two white keys are D and E. Pick a group near the middle of the keyboard.

2. **Count and tap for two minutes.** Count “1, 2, 3, 4” slowly and evenly, then repeat. Tap the same pulse. These four beats form one measure for this exercise. A metronome is optional; if you use one, try roughly 50 to 60 clicks per minute and let each click be one count. Adjust the pace to something comfortable.

3. **Play for four minutes.** In the right hand, the thumb is finger 1, index finger 2, and middle finger 3. Place them comfortably on C, D, and E. Play C-D-E-D, one note on each count. Release each key as you move to the next. Rest briefly between attempts.

4. **Learn the melody below for six minutes.** Say the notes once. Play the first group of four, then the second group. Count slowly. Once they feel familiar, try all eight notes in sequence.

5. **Improvise for three minutes.** Use C, D, and E to make a short answer to the melody. Repeat a note, leave a beat silent, or end on C. Listen to which ending you prefer.

6. **Write a two-minute check-in.** Note which movement needed the most attention and whether the count stayed even.

This melody uses one note per beat. Each group of counts 1 through 4 is one measure; keep the pulse steady as the count returns to 1.

| Beat | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Note | C | D | E | D | E | D | C | C |
| Right-hand finger | 1 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 1 |

Play each note for one beat. For the final repeated Cs, release and press C again on the next beat. Lesson 2 introduces standard notation for these notes.

## Second session · practise the transition

First try the melody from memory, then check the table. Notice where the pulse or fingering becomes uncertain.

Practise these overlapping groups at the same slow pulse:

| Group | Notes | Original beat count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Notes 1 through 5 | C D E D E | 1 2 3 4 1 |
| Notes 4 through 8 | D E D C C | 4 1 2 3 4 |
| Whole phrase | C D E D E D C C | 1 2 3 4 1 2 3 4 |

Start the second group on the spoken count “4”; it begins partway through the first measure. Keep the notes even and avoid making its first note louder just because it is your starting point.

After a few attempts, spend a minute finding Cs elsewhere on the keyboard, then return to the phrase. If counting and playing together feel too demanding, tap and count first, practise the notes separately, then combine them in a shorter group.

Then improvise: keep the first four notes and change the last four.

## Third session · recall and check

Try the phrase before reviewing the table, then play it while counting. Also try beginning at note 4. We are checking whether you can enter the phrase from more than one place.

You are ready for Lesson 2 when you can find several Cs, locate D and E beside one of them, and play this phrase at a comfortable, reasonably steady pace across separate visits. Note any repeated errors for your check-in.

An optional demonstration is [Bill Hilton's first beginner lesson on the keyboard](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QBH6IpRkVDs). Use it if the key layout or finger numbering would be clearer to see. The assignment is the exercise above.

For your check-in, describe what you practised and where you had difficulty. For example: “I tried Lesson 1 twice. Finding the notes is easy. I pause between the fourth and fifth notes. The overlapping groups helped a little. I liked inventing the ending.” Include any discomfort so we can adjust the task.
