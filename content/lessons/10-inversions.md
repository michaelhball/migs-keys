## Your goal

Play a longer accompaniment with less right-hand movement. Keep the same bass roots as Lesson 9 while rearranging the notes in the right hand.

## Rearrange a chord

C–E–G, E–G–C and G–C–E contain the same chord notes. In C major, C is the root, E the third, and G the fifth, named by counting C D E F G. In a triad by itself, putting the **third** at the bottom gives **first inversion**; putting the **fifth** at the bottom gives **second inversion**. The chord’s **root** still names it. For example, Am is always built from A–C–E, even when C is the lowest right-hand note.

A **voicing** is how chord notes are arranged and spaced. Since the left hand in this exercise still plays the root beneath the right hand, the **combined two-hand chord remains in root position**. We are using inversions of the right-hand shape to make smoother voicings. This distinction matters when analysing a full piano score.

## Hand positions

Use fingers **1–3–5** for these right-hand shapes:

| Chord | Right-hand keys | Shape by itself | Left bass with finger 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C | C4 E4 G4 | Root position | C3 |
| Am | C4 E4 A4 | First inversion | A2 |
| F | C4 F4 A4 | Second inversion | F2 |
| G | B3 D4 G4 | First inversion | G2 |

**B3** is the white key immediately left of middle C. On the treble staff it is the space just below middle C’s ledger line. Move the hand slightly for each shape. In the F shape, the thumb-to-middle-finger distance is wider than in C; do not force it. If 1–2–5 is more comfortable for C–F–A, use it consistently and make a journal note.

## Practise · about 25 minutes

1. Play each right-hand shape slowly. Name all three notes to check that changing their order has preserved the chord.
2. Alternate C and Am. Notice that C and E stay on the same keys while G moves to A. Both written chords are still rearticulated; no tie is shown.
3. Add F and G. Practise F→G and G→C without bass, then with the bass notes from Lesson 9.
4. Play bars 1–4: C–Am–F–G. As before, play together on 1 and 3, holding each half note for two beats.
5. Bars 5–12 repeat this loop twice. Keep the changes even, then learn the ending **C–F–G–C** in bars 13–16.
6. Join bars 12–13 and play the full 16-bar accompaniment. Make the final C sound like an ending by holding its full written value, then releasing together.

## Listen to the changes

**Voice leading** means how individual notes move between chords. Compare this version with Lesson 9: the chord identities stay the same, but nearby right-hand notes often move only one or two keys. A different voicing does not necessarily change the inversion of the complete harmony; listen to the lowest bass note too.

[Musictheory.net: triad inversions](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/42) shows how a triad’s lowest note determines its position. Ignore figured-bass abbreviations until we introduce them later.

## Check and continue

Play the four shapes from their chord names, then read the 16-bar score. Explain why C–E–A is still A minor and why the low A in the left hand makes the combined chord root position.
