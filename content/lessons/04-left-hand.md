## Your goal

Find a comfortable left-hand position and read three notes in the bass clef. Leave the right hand out for this exercise.

## Locate the notes and fingers

Find middle C, then the next C to its left: **C3**, one octave lower. Put your left little finger (5) on C3, ring finger (4) on D3, and middle finger (3) on E3. Your index finger (2) rests near F3 and thumb (1) near G3. Finger numbering does not reverse between hands: both thumbs are 1 and both little fingers are 5. An octave is the distance between one C and the next C.

Only fingers 5, 4 and 3 play in this exercise; do not press all five keys together. Keep the hand and wrist comfortable. If a movement feels strained, stop and adjust the position.

## Read the bass staff

The bass clef, also called the F clef, is the curled symbol with two dots. The dots sit around the F line, the second line from the top. Count all staff lines from the bottom when describing positions.

- C3 is in the **second space from the bottom**.
- D3 is on the **middle line**.
- E3 is in the **third space from the bottom**, just above D3.

These are lower notes than the C, D, and E in the treble-clef lessons. A clef changes which pitches the staff positions represent.

## Practise · about 15–20 minutes

1. **Read before playing.** Find the middle line (D3). The space just below it is C3; the space just above it is E3. Point to each note in the first bar and say its name. Then play C3, D3, and E3 separately. Use the left-hand keyboard display to check which octave is playing.
2. Read and play the first bar: C, D, E, D, using 5, 4, 3, 4. Start without the metronome if you need time to find the fingers.
3. Read and play the second bar: E, D, C, C. For the repeated C, release and press the key again on the next beat.
4. Practise notes 3–6: E, D, E, D. Keep the gaps even through the change of bar.
5. Play the whole phrase. Compare the loudness of the little, ring, and middle fingers. Aim for comfortable, reasonably even notes; do not force weak-feeling fingers.

## Listen and answer

Play C3, D3, E3 with the left hand. Answer with E3, D3, C3. Then invent a different four-note answer using these same keys. Leave a beat silent if you want.

## Check and continue

Point to C3, D3, and E3 on the staff in a different order and play them. Then play the complete melody once. If it is steady at a comfortable speed, you can try Lesson 5 today. Review this bass-clef reading briefly at your next session.
